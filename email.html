02 · Lecture 8 · Welch's t-test and p-hacking

Both emails raise revenue

Three tests, one correction

Bonferroni: reject H0 when p ≤ α/m, with m = 3 tests.

Splits the no-email group at random into two halves, 20 times. There is no real effect, so every "significant" result is a false positive.

Why we correct

Fake tests run so far0
Batches with ≥1 false positive at α = 0.05–
…with Bonferroni α/20–

With 20 tests at α = 0.05, about 1 − 0.9520 ≈ 64% of batches contain a false discovery. Bonferroni brings that back to about 5%.

03 · Lecture 11 · Beta–Binomial updating

Feed the posterior, watch it learn

04 · Lecture 9 · Nonparametric bootstrap

Split the lift: buyers × basket

Revenue per customer is μ = p · m, the share who buy times mean spend per buyer, so μT/μC = (pT/pC) · (mT/mC). No formula gives the SE of that, so we resample both arms with replacement and recompute everything.

05 · Q3 · Beta–Binomial within groups

The women's email misses men's buyers

Reading it

Groups are defined by what customers bought before randomization, so each comparison stays randomized. Points are posterior means of θemail − θcontrol with 95% credible intervals.

Exploratory: six subgroup comparisons, not planned in advance. The six Welch tests agree after a Bonferroni correction.

06 · Validation · Bootstrap vs CLT

Does the CLT survive 99% zeros?

Two standard errors, one answer

Welch's test relies on X̄T − X̄C being approximately normal. The bootstrap makes no such assumption. They agree, so with about 21,000 customers per arm the CLT holds even for this zero-heavy outcome.

Uses the replicates from chapter 04. Resample there to refresh.
