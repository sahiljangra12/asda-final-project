ASDA final project · Hillstrom email experiment · 64,000 customers randomized to three arms

Emails bring more buyers, not bigger baskets

Customers who got either email bought more often in the next two weeks, but buyers spent about the same. Every number below is recomputed in your browser from the experiment's counts.

Concept 1 · Bayesian Beta–Binomial updating (Lecture 11)

Posterior conversion rate of each arm

Prior θ ~ Beta(α0, β0); after h buyers out of n customers the posterior is Beta(α0+h, β0+n−h). Change the customers, the prior or the amount of data.

Monte Carlo from the posteriors (40,000 draws)

Concept 2 · Nonparametric bootstrap (Lecture 9)

Revenue lift = buyer effect × basket effect

Revenue per customer is μ = p · m (share who buy × mean spend per buyer), so μT/μC = (pT/pC)(mT/mC). Each replicate resamples both arms with replacement and recomputes all three ratios.

Concept 1, continued · sequential updating

How fast the evidence arrived

Customers of the men's-email and control arms were fed to the Beta update one batch at a time, in random order. The line is P(θmen > θctrl | data so far). Processing data sequentially gives the same final posterior as processing it all at once.

Concept 3 · Welch's t-test and p-hacking (Lecture 8)

Revenue per customer: three tests, one correction

Welch's statistic TW = (X̄T − X̄C) / √(ST2/nT + SC2/nC). Bonferroni multiplies each p-value by the number of tests (3). The men's-vs-women's difference does not survive the correction.
